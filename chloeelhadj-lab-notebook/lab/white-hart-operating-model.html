The White Hart Operating Model

Guests experience a hotel as one seamless stay. Behind the scenes, that experience depends on hundreds of small decisions shared across the front desk, housekeeping, maintenance, the restaurant, and management..

Why the White Hart?

The White Hart is a boutique inn in Salisbury, Connecticut. Its relatively small scale makes the organization understandable while still containing the operational complexity of a much larger hotel. Because departments interact constantly, it provides an ideal setting for studying how information—not hierarchy—coordinates daily operations.

Traditional organizational charts describe reporting relationships. This model instead describes information relationships.

The project argues that boutique hotels create value by continuously transforming new information into coordinated human decisions. The Front Desk emerges not as the center of authority, but as the organization's primary coordination hub.

Applying the Model

The operating model above is abstract by design. What follows makes it concrete: a daily-use dashboard, the technical layer beneath it, one ticket traced through the whole system, a capture tool for informal knowledge, and the closing image of what organizational memory actually looks like.

The operating model explains how information moves through the hotel. The Morning View asks a different question: If this model were visible to a manager at 8:00 a.m., what would they actually see?

Large systems become understandable by following one piece of information. This workflow traces a single maintenance issue from guest request to resolution, revealing each handoff, decision point, and opportunity for information loss.

The most valuable information inside a boutique hotel often exists nowhere in software. It lives in experienced employees: recurring guests, room quirks, trusted vendors, seasonal patterns, and unwritten service standards. This institutional knowledge is both the hotel's competitive advantage and its greatest operational risk.

Key Insights

01 — The Front Desk is the hotel's primary coordination hub.

02 — Most operational friction occurs during information handoffs.

03 — Institutional knowledge is operational infrastructure.

04 — AI is most valuable when retrieving and coordinating information, not replacing human judgment.

Reflection

I began this project expecting to design an AI tool. Instead, I ended up studying organizations. The most important insight wasn't where AI fits—it was how much of a hotel's success depends on information that is shared through people rather than software. Understanding that operating system became more interesting than automating it.
